There are only a few things I think  you've missed in the scenario and I'm obviously less than sure how they would affect it. I mention them in order of potential impact:


> (TL;DR: The war in Ukraine will not only have geopolitical impact, but is already accelerating military AI dev in EU countries. Increased climate will not be a separate trajectory, but collide with AI resource guzzling. Omställningsfonder will be copied across the EU. And like the release of ChatGPT, we'll see a new Black Swan during this timeframe.) 

## a) The war in Ukraine
It's mentioned in the "history" part, but then it's almost never mentioned in the scenario. Only by "proxy", when Baltic and Nordic countries are worried about the Russians. Since the Russian economy is smaller than combined ditto of the Nordics, it's not a long shot that a prolonged war will weaken Russia considerably. And apart from the geopolitcal impact on the EU, for this scenario the war has a very hands on effect on AI. 

Just look at the autonomous drone swarm system SAAB built during the winter that can either fly together with its "mother drone", like a larger, modern Reaper, or with a manned fighter jet. The onboard, military AI models are very advanced, without using frontier LLM:s. And this points to a potential development fork we'll come back to. (Obviously the aftermath of the war in Iran and Lebanon will also have traction - since, sadly, war is the mother of inventions and not necessity.) 

## b) Climate Change
Again, we have alread in mid June an intense heat wave in France and other EU countries. (Even authorities in Nordic countries released a severe weather, heat warning last week.) This plus floods and shortened winter seasons, well, you're unfortunately to familiar with all the different climate impacts across the EU. And since hyperscaling compute is putting large dents (as you document) in energy production and global AI demand in 2027 is projected to account for 4.2–6.6 trillion liters of water we can put the two and two together. 

There's going to be a very real impact from scaling and then there's going to be a great public opinion problem for scalers that's separate from the labour issues: The voters are going to ask politicians if they will give their citizens water or let the scalers keep guzzling.

There's also potential upsides for this factor: The push in the Nordics for green energy and manufacturing will give Europe a competitive edge when it comes to datacenters with cheap energy in volumes from hydro/nuclear/hydrogen.  

## c) Omställningsstöd
Omställningsstöd. Labour insurance is mentioned, and there's a Danish example. But the large systems started in Norway as well as Sweden (maybe Finland too?) already in the 1950s where the government, the industry and the unions all deposit large chunks each month in 'omställningsfonden' - transition support funds. Right now in Sweden, you can get 80% of your salary if you are between 27 and 63 and want to study something that updates your skillset. Since the EU members are good at benchmarking each other, this system will be adapted in more countries and not only mitigate labour crises, but actually produce 'new talent". Sweden is historically a great example, moving from a large manufacturing industry from the end of WWII to a majority of white collar employees and fast digitalization in the 1990s, basically manufacturing new kind of jobs. The tight collaboration between unions and industry, a.k.a. "the Swedish Model", was they key and will have a potential outsize impact in the AI transition. 

## d) The Black Swan
The scenario is really engagingly narrated and using the setup of telling a fictional story from the point of view of believable characters is a great tool. What's perhaps missing, if you compare with the Oxford Scenario Model, is the two other scenarios... :-) Obviously I realize that could've made the exercise too immense, as well as the reading. But there's one thing I think could go into the ending from the 'Oxford methodology'. 

Because it's the one scenario, it becomes a bit linear and even Carolines thoughts on what should've been done during the interview are 'locked inside' this specific universe. This makes it verge on forecasting. But if we combine Taleb's "The Black Swan" with a little AI history and the military models from SAAB, what do we get? You already know all about tensorflow, transformers and the development of the LLM:s. But very few (except insiders at OpenAI, DeepMind, etc.) predicted ChatGPT when GPT-2 was withdrawn in 2019. And if hyperscaling means that LLM models in the end can't get better even when they have all the energy and all the water on the planet? So obviously we'll have another 'ChatGPT & Claude Code moment' during your timeframe. SAABs onboard models which, for obvious reasons, can't use US based LLMs, point to one trajectory: Combining well trained agents with expert systems. Or we'll see somthing completely different.

In the same way people during the dotcom crash couldn't see the dynamic web of blogs, CMSs and social media even when they were just six months away.

Europes mission, should it choose to accept it, isn't to play catch up in the hyperscaling race. It's to invent the next Black Swan of AI.
