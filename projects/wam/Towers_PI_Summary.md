# Towers PI Planning: aggregated notes

Sources: typed notes (Solution Management, ART Review Day 2, Management Review) and notebook pages 1–4. Full detail in `Towers_PI_Notes_combined.md`.

## Summary

The PI planning made capacity problems visible before they occur: people can now see that they are overbooked in advance rather than when it happens. The main problems are in how capacity is planned and how dependencies are agreed. Teams plan per individual and in hours, not per team and against objectives, which takes longer and hides the team view. Dependencies are recorded as accepted in the Excel sheet without the handshake conversation behind them, and some requests to other Solutions were turned down because those teams were busy planning, although PI planning is the event for those handshakes. A small number of people (TCM, competence leads, CAD) carry demand from several teams, and some people work on five projects at once. Roles are part of the cause: POs are not yet taking ownership of prioritisation, and guidance, templates and tooling (Jira, Confluence, Excel, Miro) are unclear. Team presentations were too long.

## 1. Capacity and allocation
- Planned allocation sits around 80–90%, and the last sprint is empty.
- Teams hold capacity "just in case", which blocks other work. There is no generic project to book placeholder hours against, and project budget vs. capacity placeholders is unresolved.
- Overload is concentrated on individuals: one person in Team 1, TCM resources in Team 2 (already fully booked), people on up to five projects at once.
- Team 5 has 200 hours of critical demand; Team 4 is providing new support to cover part of it, possibly if the SM caps other work.
- Hours are estimated conservatively.

## 2. Planning per individual instead of per team
- Teams discussed individuals and individual hours, and estimated individually instead of as a team (Team 1, PI planning observations, Summary).
- Planning per individual takes longer than planning per team.
- Teams are not working with sprint or PI objectives (Team 1). Team 5 started from an objective; Team 3 discussed objectives with one person halfway through, not with the whole team.
- Open question: how are the teams constituted, if capacity is tracked per task and per person?

## 3. Dependencies and handshakes
- Dependency allocation is missing at Solution level, and dependency tracking/agreement was the main problem raised in the ART review.
- Dependencies are marked as accepted in the Excel sheet without the handshake conversation (Team 1).
- Requests to teams in other Solutions were rejected because those teams were busy planning (Team 2 and Summary).
- Known dependencies: TCM (Teams 1 and 2), Procurement NDA (Team 2), control/hardware for test timeline – interface requirements, damper 3D modelling (Team 2), LAQ (Teams 2 and 4), Competence Lead in ART 1 and ART 2/Platform (Team 5), Denis's team (Team 3), CAD resources (Management Review).
- Pre-alignment with procurement and building is in place.

## 4. Unplanned and external demand
- Regions send ISM requests on Friday and expect work to start Monday; they do not know the sprint cadence (Team 4).
- Cross-functional teams add tasks to other teams' Jira; Team 3's backlog is mostly tasks from another team that the team cannot explain.
- System disturbances and interruptions of ongoing work need to be anticipated (Team 3).
- "Not a project for my team so I will not book it" (Team 1).

## 5. Roles and responsibility
- POs need to take responsibility for prioritisation; awareness of this is low. Open question: how do you tell someone to take less?
- The RTE should move between team breakouts and support them; coaches should not talk directly to POs and SMs but route through the RTE.
- SM role in aligning new value is unclear (Team 3).
- Too many depend on too few people, including for training and competence ownership.
- The ART cannot sign off on problems or agree on its own tooling.

## 6. Governance, terminology and tooling
- Lack of guidance and governance; a template is needed.
- Confusion about which tool is used for what (Confluence, Jira, Excel, Miro). Suggestion from Team 1: write in Confluence before Jira, and visualise capacity.
- Epic vs. Feature terminology is unclear (Team 2).
- Requirements are part of Flow Agile and aligned with the cadence, but not aligned with PRs.

## 7. Event format
- Team presentations are too long.
- Proposed focus for the review: dependencies, impediments and risks rather than individuals (Team 2). Each team to present objectives, dependencies and risks (Team 3).
- Risks could be stated as part of the objectives (Team 2), and working from assumptions was suggested instead of a strictly risk-based plan (Team 1).

## 9. Positive results
- People see overbooking in advance.
- Opportunities to address it are now up front.
- Pre-alignment with procurement and building.
- Teams 3, 4 and 5 have plans updated in Jira.

## 10. Open questions and follow-ups
- Where to book placeholder and "just in case" hours (generic project?).
- How to move from individual to team capacity and estimation.
- How to make dependency acceptance require a handshake, including across Solutions.
- How to bring Regions' ISM requests into the cadence.
- Whether someone inside the ART can cover the Competence Lead dependency (Team 5).
- Tooling decision: which tool holds what.
- Shorter review format focused on objectives, dependencies and risks.
